## <span id="fukuoka">[Fukuoka](#fukuoka)</span>

We started in Fukuoka, the birthplace of tonkotsu ramen, our gateway into Kyushu. I think Hakata felt even more laid back than Osaka and Tokyo, good walks, and easy to go around.

Ramen was the priority. We went looking for a proper Hakata bowl: thin noodles, rich tonkotsu broth, and that first sip that resets the whole day. It delivered.

We also joined a few tours around the city, including a stop at the reclining Buddha and a stretch of seaside. The Buddha was calm and huge up close, the kind of place that slows you down without trying. By the water, Fukuoka felt open and breezy, a softer side of the trip before we headed deeper into Kyushu.

## <span id="sapporo">[Sapporo](#sapporo)</span>

From Kyushu we jumped north to Sapporo, and the whole feel of the trip shifted again.

Mt. Moiwa gave us the wide view over the city, worth the climb up for that lookout moment when everything finally opens. We also visited the Royce chocolate factory, which felt like a fun, very Hokkaido stop between the heavier and quieter parts of the itinerary.

One of the more unexpected highlights was Makomanai Takino Cemetery. The giant Buddha there is striking on its own, but the grounds also had this Stonehenge replica and other stone monuments scattered through the landscape. It felt surreal and peaceful at the same time.

We wrapped the Sapporo stretch with Sapporo TV Tower, a clean city landmark to close out the northern leg before heading back to Fukuoka for the Nagasaki trip.

## <span id="nagasaki">[Nagasaki](#nagasaki)</span>

Nagasaki hit differently. The pace was quieter, and the history sat closer to the surface.

We visited the 26 Martyrs Memorial, standing at a site that carries centuries of weight. Then we went to the Atomic Bomb Museum. It was heavy, carefully presented, and hard to walk through quickly. Even after leaving, it stayed with us, one of those places that changes how you look at the rest of the day.

We also visited Nakamachi church where we had to remove our shoes to go inside, it was really nice. It was only us at that time and I think that is how it was always done as there are large shoe racks at the entrance where visitors are supposed to put their shoes.

## <span id="thoughts">[Thoughts](#thoughts)</span>

This trip stretched Japan both ways, Kyushu south, Hokkaido north. Fukuoka was easy living and tonkotsu. Sapporo was cold air and wide views. Nagasaki slowed us down.

As an experienced developer, I keep thinking about making this more than a trip. Live here with my wife. Learn the language. Find an employer who can sponsor an engineer visa.

Back home, infrastructure is lagging hard. We're paying first-world prices for a developing-country quality of life, and even that phrase is too soft.

I don't see that gap closing anytime soon. Japan keeps showing what it feels like when things actually work. Hard not to want that for real.
