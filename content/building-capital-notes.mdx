I have been investing for years, and the hardest part is not finding information. It is keeping decisions honest over time.

I would read earnings, run a valuation, feel confident about a position, and six months later forget why I sized it the way I did. Spreadsheets tracked holdings. Notes apps held fragments. Nothing connected portfolio state, research, and the reasoning behind each call.

That gap is what I am building toward with **Capital Notes**.

<br />

## <span id="what-it-is">[What it is](#what-it-is)</span>

Capital Notes is a research and analysis notebook for your portfolio. It helps investors turn portfolio data, market research, and AI-assisted analysis into a structured decision journal.

The core loop is simple:

- Enter holdings and cash
- Define your portfolio thesis and risk limits (optional)
- Run structured analyses against live portfolio context
- Save results as notes with a full audit trail

Instead of scattered docs and half-remembered conviction, every decision has a timestamp, a confidence score, and the reasoning that produced it.

<br />

## <span id="what-is-in-the-app">[What is in the app](#what-is-in-the-app)</span>

The app is organized around three surfaces:

**Portfolio dashboard** — Holdings, cash, concentration, and theme exposure at a glance. You see total value, position weights, and returns without leaving the app.

**AI analysis** — Structured prompts, not open-ended chat. Health checks, portfolio reviews, exposure assessments, sentiment analysis, stock comparisons, and customer discovery. Each analysis type has a defined output: recommendations, risk framing, and decision-oriented takeaways.

**Decision journal** — Saved analysis notes, watchlist items, and follow-up runs. When you revisit a ticker months later, you can see what you thought, when you thought it, and what changed.

Settings round out the context: a portfolio thesis (north star, strategic intent, constraints) and risk limits that feed into every analysis run.

<br />

## <span id="why-structured-analysis">[Why structured analysis](#why-structured-analysis)</span>

Most investing tools either show you data or let you chat with a model. Neither solves the journal problem.

Raw data does not record intent. Unstructured chat does not create a durable record you can compare against later.

Capital Notes sits in the middle. Analyses are typed, labeled, and saved. A portfolio health check from March and a follow-up in June are linked by ticker and kind, not buried in a conversation thread.

The goal is not to replace your judgment. It is to make your judgment legible — to you, six months from now.

<br />

## <span id="stack">[Stack](#stack)</span>

On the engineering side, it is a straightforward modern web app:

- Next.js 16 (App Router) + TypeScript
- PostgreSQL + Drizzle ORM
- better-auth for email/password and optional Google OAuth
- Kimi K2.6 via the Vercel AI SDK
- Polar for subscription billing

The UI takes cues from Linear — dark panels, tight typography, minimal chrome. I want the product to feel a bit of a research tool, not a consumer fintech app.

<br />

## <span id="where-things-stand">[Where things stand](#where-things-stand)</span>

The core loop works end to end: sign up, seed a portfolio, run an analysis, save a note, revisit it later.

What is still ahead: polish on the marketing site, tighter prompt iteration as I use the product on my own portfolio.

I am building this because I need it. If the decision journal problem resonates with you, I will share more as the project evolves.
